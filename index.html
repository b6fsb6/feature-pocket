Microphone level

Hit the pad. The bar should cross the line on every stroke and fall back under it between strokes. Raise the line if room noise triggers hits, lower it if soft strokes are missed. Echo cancellation and noise suppression are switched off for this page so stick attacks stay sharp.

Stick calibration

Each stick sounds a little different. Calibrate by hitting the same spot of the pad with one stick only, at your normal playing strength. The app learns the tone of each stick and then flags strokes where the wrong hand seems to have played.

Latency

Sound reaches the page late, and the click reaches your ears late. This number removes that delay. Calibrate shows a big countdown, then plays the same row of 16 clicks three times, labelled Round 1 of 3, 2 of 3 and 3 of 3, with a cursor on the row. Hit the pad right on each click. It assumes you were on the beat on average, so your own lead or drag is measured against that.

How it works

The app plays a click at your tempo and listens to your pad through the microphone. It matches each hit you play to the stroke that was due, then measures how many milliseconds early (leading) or late (dragging) it was. Nothing is uploaded. Everything stays in this browser.

Pick a sticking, press Start on the Play page, and play along with the moving line. Your strokes light up as you play, and the numbers appear when you stop.

Quick start

  1. Setup: pick a tempo, use headphones for the click, press "Turn microphone on" and hit the pad. The level bar should cross the trigger line on every stroke. Calibrating each stick is optional but lets the app tell L from R.
  2. Pattern: choose "One pattern" (a single sticking that repeats) or "Practice page" (a list of bars played one after another).
  3. Play: press Start. After a count-in, play on the moving line. Press Stop when you are done to see your results.

No microphone? On Setup, change Input to "Keyboard or pads". Press F for left and J for right. This is a test mode, and its timing is rougher than a microphone on a pad.

The four pages

  • Play: Start, Pause, Restart and Stop, the notation, the lead/drag gauge and your results. With a practice page loaded, scroll or swipe the rows (or press ↑ ↓) to choose which bar to start at.
  • Pattern: what you practise: one pattern, or a page of bars you type, load from a file or load from a URL.
  • Setup: tempo, click, on-time window, microphone, stick calibration and latency.
  • Progress: your scores for each pattern, weakest first, and each page's best cleared tempo.

Reading the Play page

  • Each row is one pass of a bar, the way it is played. The top row is the one you are playing, and the next passes sit underneath. A bar with 16 passes shows the same notation 16 times as it comes round. When a pass ends the rows slide up.
  • The moving line glides across the row at an even pace and reaches a note exactly when it is due. Strike as the line reaches the note.
  • Letter colours: green is on time, blue is early (leading), pink is late (dragging), and a faded letter means no hit was heard. The "on-time window" in Setup decides how close counts as green.
  • A small arrow tag such as →R beside a letter means that stroke was due on the other hand but the app heard R. This needs both sticks calibrated.
  • The gauge shows your average lead or drag over the last 32 strokes. Left of the middle is leading and right is dragging.
  • On narrow screens a busy pass wraps onto two lines at the barline so the notes stay roomy.
  • Pause stops the click. Resume counts you in again from the start of that pass. Restart goes back to your chosen start bar.

Writing a pattern

L and R are strokes. A space starts a new group, and groups are joined with a beam. _ is a rest. A /N marker sets how many strokes fit in one beat from that point on: /1 quarters, /2 eighths, /3 eighth triplets, /4 sixteenths, up to /6. With no marker, the "Each stroke is a" setting on the Pattern page is used (sixteenths by default).

Every example below has a button that loads it for you.

Four beats of sixteenths

RLRL RLRL RLRL RLRL

Four groups of four. Each group is one beat. The notation shows a barline every two beats.

Accent patterns

RLRR LRLL RLRR LRLL

Set the click to "Every stroke" in Setup to hear each note, or leave it "On the beat".

Two triplets in one beat

/3 RLR LRL

Two groups of three, each with its own "3". Triplets are drawn with a bracket and the click accents each beat.

Sixteenths then sextuplets

/4 RLRL /6 RLR LRL

One beat of sixteenths, then one beat of sextuplets written as two joined triplets (two "3" brackets). Writing /6 RLRLRL with no space instead gives one run of six, with a single "6".

Rests

/4 R _ L _ R L _ L

A rest takes up time but no hit is expected. A hit during a rest counts as an extra hit.

The practice page format

A practice page is plain text with one bar per line. Lines starting with # are notes to yourself, and a name before a colon (Bar 4:) is just a label. This app does not come with any book content. You supply the bars.

title: My warm-up page
each: 1m
RLRR LRLL
RLRL RLRL  x16
LRLL RLRR  90s  @100
Bar 4: RRLL RRLL
/4 RLRL /3 RLR LRL

How long each bar plays

  • each: sets the default for every bar: 1m (a minute), 90s or x8 (eight passes). With no each: line, the "Each bar plays for" setting is used.
  • x16 at the end of a bar's line means play this bar 16 times. One pass is the bar played once through.
  • 90s or 1.5m means play for about that long. Time is rounded to whole passes.
  • @100 plays just that bar at 100 BPM. Combine them freely: RLRL RLRL x16 @80.

Passes: how x16 works

title: Passes demo
each: x4
RLRL RLRL RLRL RLRL
LRLR LRLR LRLR LRLR  x8
RRLL RRLL RRLL RRLL  30s

At 60 BPM one pass of each bar takes 4 seconds. The first bar gets the page default of 4 passes, the second gets 8 passes, and the third plays for 30 seconds, which is 7 passes.

Between bars and the bar-change cue

"Between bars" can be a 4-beat count-in, or Straight on (the default), where the next bar starts without a pause. With Straight on, a cue tells you a change is coming: one high tick on the first beat of a bar's last pass, then two ticks and a flash on the first beat of the new bar. The "Bar-change cue" setting turns the sound or the flash off.

Loading a page

Use Load file for a .txt, .json or .csv, or Load from URL. JSON also works: a list such as ["RLRR LRLL", {"sticking":"RLRL RLRL","passes":16}], or an object with title, each and bars.

Scores, tempo and progress

  • On time is the share of due strokes that landed inside your on-time window. A bar needs at least one full pass to be scored.
  • Bar table: after a practice page run, each bar gets its own accuracy, average error and spread, plus your earlier best at that tempo.
  • The target is 90%. A bar is "cleared" at a tempo when it reaches 90% there. After a run the app says whether every bar cleared, and suggests +4 BPM if so. Otherwise it names the weak bars and suggests staying at the same tempo. These are only suggestions. Any tempo is always allowed.
  • Discard this run removes a run from your records, for example after a bad microphone read.
  • Progress page: every pattern, weakest first, with accuracy at the tempo you last played, your fastest cleared tempo and your lead/drag bias. "Practise" loads that pattern. A page counts as cleared at a tempo when one run covered every bar and all reached 90%.
  • Records are saved in this browser only. Use Export to keep a copy or move to another device, and Import to bring one back.

Setup details

  • Trigger line: raise it if room noise counts as hits, and lower it if soft strokes are missed.
  • Stick calibration: hit the same spot of the pad with one stick only, at normal strength, until the count finishes. Do it for both sticks.
  • Latency: sound reaches the page late, and the click reaches your ears late. Press Calibrate latency, then hit right on each click for three rounds. It assumes you were on the click on average. You can also type a number.
  • Click: "On the beat", "Every stroke" or "Count-in only". Use headphones, or the click will be picked up as strokes.

If something is off

  • The microphone will not turn on: the page needs a secure address. Opening the file from your computer works in Chrome and Edge. Otherwise serve it with python3 -m http.server or switch Input to Keyboard.
  • Every stroke looks late or early: run Calibrate latency.
  • Extra hits show up: raise the trigger line, or use headphones for the click.
  • Wrong-hand tags look random: calibrate both sticks again on the same spot of the pad.
