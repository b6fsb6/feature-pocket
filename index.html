Tempo and click

Use headphones for the click. A click from the speakers is picked up by the microphone and counted as a stroke.

Input

Test mode. Use it to try the page without a microphone. Phone and keyboard timing is rougher than a mic on a pad.

Microphone level

Hit the pad. The bar should cross the line on every stroke and fall back under it between strokes. Raise the line if room noise triggers hits, lower it if soft strokes are missed. Echo cancellation and noise suppression are switched off for this page so stick attacks stay sharp.

Stick calibration

Each stick sounds a little different. Calibrate by hitting the same spot of the pad with one stick only, at your normal playing strength. The app learns the tone of each stick and then flags strokes where the wrong hand seems to have played.

Latency

Sound reaches the page late, and the click reaches your ears late. This number removes that delay. Calibrate shows a big countdown, then plays the same row of 16 clicks three times, labelled Round 1 of 3, 2 of 3 and 3 of 3, with a cursor on the row. Hit the pad right on each click. It assumes you were on the beat on average, so your own lead or drag is measured against that.

Tap a bar to start there.

File format

Plain text, one bar per line. L and R are strokes, and a space starts a new group. With the click on "Every stroke", the start of the bar gets the strongest click and the first stroke of each group a lighter one, so 7/8 written RL RL RLR is heard as 2+2+3. Lines starting with # are notes to yourself. This page does not come with any book content. You supply the bars.

title: My warm-up page
each: 1m
RLRR LRLL
RLRL RLRL  x16
LRLL RLRR  90s  @100
Bar 4: RRLL RRLL
/4 RLRL /3 RLR LRL
R L _ L R _ L R

each: sets the default time for every bar (1m, 90s or x8 for eight passes). A bar can override it with x16, 90s or 1.5m at the end of its line, and can set its own tempo with @100. Bars without any of these use the setting above. Time is rounded to whole passes. A name before a colon (Bar 4:) is just a label.

/N sets the subdivision for the strokes that follow, until the next marker: /1 quarters, /2 eighths, /3 triplets, /4 sixteenths (up to /6). So /4 RLRL /3 RLR LRL is one beat of sixteenths then two beats of triplets. Strokes before any marker use the "Each stroke is a" setting below. A marker can start a line to set the whole bar.

_ is a rest, and so is a lone - with spaces around it (R - L -). A rest takes up time like a stroke, and the click still counts it, but no hit is expected. A hit during a rest counts as an extra hit. A hyphen joined to strokes (RLRR-LRLL) just separates groups.

Bar-change cue. With "Straight on" there is no count-in between bars, so the page gives its own cue. On the first beat of a bar's last pass you hear one high tick. On the first beat of the new bar you hear two ticks and the strokes flash. The ticks sound whatever the click is set to. They are only used with "Straight on". The next bar always sits under the current one, and the rows shift up when it starts.

JSON also works: a list such as ["RLRR LRLL", {"sticking":"RLRL RLRL","passes":16}], or an object with title, each and bars.
