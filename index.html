Microphone level

    Settle time is how long the app ignores the pad after a hit. Raise it if one stroke counts twice. Fast playing lowers it automatically so real strokes are not skipped. Mic check listens to the room, then to ten strokes, and sets both for you.

    Hit the pad. The bar should cross the line on every stroke and fall back under it between strokes. Raise the line if room noise triggers hits, lower it if soft strokes are missed. Echo cancellation and noise suppression are switched off for this page so stick attacks stay sharp.

    Stick calibration

      Test left vs right asks for 20 single hits, one hand at a time in a shuffled order, and shows how often the app named the right stick. Calibrate both sticks first.

      Each stick sounds a little different. Calibrate by hitting the same spot of the pad with one stick only, at your normal playing strength. The app learns the tone of each stick and then flags strokes where the wrong hand seems to have played.

      Latency

      Estimate it

      Auto calibrate (recommended): plays 12 clicks through the speakers and times when the mic hears them. You don't play. Use speakers, not headphones, and keep the room quiet. It measures the device delay only.

      Calibrate by playing: counts you in, then loops clicks. Hit the pad right on each one and watch the number settle, then stop and it finishes by itself. It needs 80 hits and assumes you were on the beat on average. Use it to fine-tune, or when you only have headphones.

      Sound reaches the page late, and the click reaches your ears late. This number removes that delay. The result of a calibration appears here.

      How it works

      The app plays a click at your tempo and listens to your pad through the microphone. It matches each hit you play to the stroke that was due, then measures how many milliseconds early (leading) or late (dragging) it was. Nothing is uploaded. Everything stays in this browser.

      Pick a sticking, press Start on the Play page, and play along with the moving line. Your strokes light up as you play, and the numbers appear when you stop.

      Quick start

      1. Settings: pick a tempo, use headphones for the click, press "Turn microphone on" and hit the pad. The level bar should cross the trigger line on every stroke. Calibrating each stick is optional but lets the app tell L from R.
      2. Pattern: choose "Pattern" (a single sticking that repeats) or "Routine" (a list of exercises played one after another). The Play page has the same switch. In Routine, it also holds how long each exercise plays, the gap between them, and where to start.
      3. Play: set the tempo and click level, then press Start. In Pattern mode, Repeats says how many times to play it, and 0 keeps going until you press Stop. The page controls hide while you play. After a count-in, play on the moving line. Press Stop when you are done. The page stays in focus mode and shows your result first, with Again, Next tempo (when you cleared the target) and Done. The staff below it keeps its colours and numbers so you can see which strokes were off. Press Done to bring the controls back.

      No microphone? In Settings, under Device, change Input to "Keyboard or pads". Press F for left and J for right. This is a test mode, and its timing is rougher than a microphone on a pad.

      The four pages

      • Play: the Pattern or Routine switch, tempo, click level, Start, Pause, Restart and Stop, the notation, the lead/drag gauge and your results. With a routine loaded, scroll or swipe the rows (or press ↑ ↓) to choose which exercise to start at.
      • Pattern: what you practise: one pattern, or a routine of exercises you type, load from a file or load from a URL.
      • Settings: Preferences has the on-time window, visual pulse and click volume. Device has the input, microphone level, stick calibration and latency. Data has export, import and clear.
      • Stats: three views. History lists your last runs by exercise or routine, with Replay. Scores shows each pattern and routine, weakest first, with its best cleared tempo. Achievements shows your practice days for the last four weeks, a streak that forgives one rest day a week, and your badges.

      Reading the Play page

      • Each row is one pass of an exercise, the way it is played. The top row is the one you are playing, and the next passes sit underneath. An exercise with 16 passes shows the same notation 16 times as it comes round. When a pass ends the rows slide up.
      • The moving line glides across the row at an even pace and reaches a note exactly when it is due. Strike as the line reaches the note.
      • Letter colours: green is on time, blue is early (leading), pink is late (dragging), and a faded letter means no hit was heard. The "on-time window" in Settings decides how close counts as green.
      • A small arrow tag such as →R beside a letter means that stroke was due on the other hand but the app heard R. This needs both sticks calibrated.
      • The gauge shows your average lead or drag over the last 32 strokes. Left of the middle is leading and right is dragging.
      • On narrow screens a busy pass wraps onto two lines at the barline so the notes stay roomy.
      • Pause stops the click. Resume counts you in again from the start of that pass. Restart goes back to your chosen start exercise.

      Writing a pattern

      L and R are strokes. A space starts a new group, and groups are joined with a beam. _ is a rest. A note code says what the strokes are, and it means the same in any time signature: q quarter notes, 8 eighths, 16 sixteenths, 32 thirty-seconds, 8t eighth-note triplets, 16t sixteenth-note triplets, 5t quintuplets and 7t septuplets. A code applies to the strokes after it until the next code, so 8 RLRL 16 RLRL is a beat of eighths, then a beat of sixteenths, in 4/4 or in cut time. A code only works where the strokes come out at a sensible speed for the beat. With no code, strokes are sixteenths

      Every example below has a button that loads it for you.

      Four beats of sixteenths

      RLRL RLRL RLRL RLRL

      Four groups of four. Each group is one beat. The notation shows a barline every two beats.

      Accent patterns

      RLRR LRLL RLRR LRLL

      Choose the click level on the Play page: Easy clicks every stroke, Medium every beat, Hard on 1 and 3, Extreme on 1 only.

      Two triplets in one beat

      8t RLR LRL

      Two groups of three, each with its own "3". Triplets are drawn with a bracket and the click accents each beat.

      Sixteenths then sextuplets

      16 RLRL 16t RLR LRL

      One beat of sixteenths, then one beat of sextuplets written as two joined triplets (two "3" brackets). Writing 16t RLRLRL with no space instead gives one run of six, with a single "6".

      Rests

      16 R _ L _ R L _ L

      A rest takes up time but no hit is expected. A hit during a rest counts as an extra hit.

      The routine format

      You do not have to type any of this. The notation field on the Editor page shows what you have written. With Type you edit it as text. With Build it is the same field but read-only, and the keys under it add to it. The speaker beside it opens a popup with the staff and plays the exercise as clicks, with a bar moving along the staff as each stroke sounds. Press the play or stop button in the popup to hear it again, and Close or Esc to leave it. In Build: pick a note code, then tap R, L, rest and space. Time and Grouping sit above the toggle and work in both. The page opens on Type, and remembers your last choice on each device. In a routine, Title, Time, Grouping and Strokes are are shared by every exercise. Insert above or Insert below, Clone and Delete manage the list, the arrow buttons beside the list move the exercise you are editing to the top, up, down or the bottom, and the arrows pick which exercise you are editing. Typing and tapping can be mixed freely, and the speaker button plays the exercise as clicks.

      A routine is plain text with one exercise per line. Lines starting with # are notes to yourself, and a name before a colon (Exercise 4:) is just a label. This app does not come with any book content. You supply the exercises.

      title: My warm-up routine
each: 1m
RLRR LRLL
RLRL RLRL  x16
LRLL RLRR  90s  @100
Exercise 4: RRLL RRLL
16 RLRL 8t RLR LRL

      How long each exercise plays

      • each: sets the default for every exercise: 1m (a minute), 90s or x8 (eight passes). With no each: line, the "Each exercise plays for" setting is used.
      • strokes: sets what the strokes are for every exercise after it, so you do not repeat a marker on each line: strokes: 8, strokes: 16 or strokes: 8t. A code inside an exercise still wins, and strokes: off goes back to sixteenths.
      • x16 at the end of an exercise's line means play this exercise 16 times. One pass is the exercise played once through.
      • 90s or 1.5m means play for about that long. Time is rounded to whole passes.
      • @100 plays just that exercise at 100 BPM. Combine them freely: RLRL RLRL x16 @80.

      Passes: how x16 works

      title: Passes demo
each: x4
RLRL RLRL RLRL RLRL
LRLR LRLR LRLR LRLR  x8
RRLL RRLL RRLL RRLL  30s

      At 60 BPM one pass of each exercise takes 4 seconds. The first exercise gets the routine default of 4 passes, the second gets 8 passes, and the third plays for 30 seconds, which is 7 passes.

      Time signatures

      By default an exercise is as long as the strokes in it. Add a time signature to say how long it should be. Write it with a colon, like 7/8:, at the start of a line, or on its own line as time: 7/8 to set it for every exercise after it. time: off goes back to plain exercises, and an exercise's own signature wins over the routine one.

      • The pulse is the beat you count. In x/4 it is a quarter note, in x/8 an eighth note and in x/2 a half note, so 2/2 is cut time. BPM counts pulses, so 7/8 at 120 BPM is 120 eighth notes a minute and 2/2 at 60 BPM is 60 half notes a minute, which is how a cut-time book marks its tempo. The summary says "per eighth note" or "per half note" when that applies. Only /2, /4 and /8 are supported.
      • Codes mean what they say. 8 is eighth notes in any signature. In 7/8, RLRLRLR with no code is seven eighth notes: with no code, the exercise's strokes are shared out evenly, so 14 strokes in 7/8 become sixteenths.
      • Grouping. 7/8 is felt as 2+2+3, 6/8 as 3+3, 5/4 as 3+2. Write your own in brackets: 7/8 (3+2+2):. The click is strong on the first pulse and lighter at the start of each group, and the notation is beamed the same way. A line with its own spaces keeps your spacing. The groups have to add up to the top number: 7/8 (3+2+2) is fine, 7/8 (3+2) is refused with a note. The builder's Grouping menu offers the common choices for 5/4, 6/8, 7/8 and 7/4, and the usual one is marked.
      • The count-in is one measure of the signature, with the same accents.
      • The exercise has to add up. It can be one bar or a whole number of bars, such as two bars of 2/2, with a barline between them and a strong click on each bar's first pulse. If the strokes do not fill the signature, a note appears, such as "this exercise holds 5 eighth notes but 7/8 has 7". Use _ for rests. The exercise still plays as you wrote it.

      A cut-time book page

      title: Cut time
time: 2/2
strokes: 8
RLRL RLRL RLRL RLRL  x8
LRLR LRLR LRLR LRLR  x8

      Two bars of eighth notes, written the way the page reads. The ¢ sign means the beat is a half note, so BPM is the book's half-note tempo.

      Between exercises and the visual pulse

      "Between exercises" can be a 4-beat count-in, or Straight on (the default), where the next exercise starts without a pause. With Straight on, nothing marks the change except the notation: the rows slide up and the new exercise's first pass starts on the next beat.

      Visual pulse (in Settings, under Preferences) makes the moving line swell on every click, bigger on the first beat of each pass, so you can see the click as well as hear it. Turn it off for a plain moving line.

      Loading a routine

      Use Save file to keep the routine as a .txt you can load again later. Use Load file for a .txt, .json or .csv, or Load from URL. JSON also works: a list such as ["RLRR LRLL", {"sticking":"RLRL RLRL","passes":16}], or an object with title, each and exercises.

      The routine library

      On the Pattern screen, in Routine mode, you can point the app at a library: a web address that lists routine files. Press Load list, then tap a routine to load it into the editor. The address is remembered, and the list is fetched when you open the Routine screen. Remove forgets it. This app does not come with any routines. You choose the library.

      The list file

      A plain text file with one routine per line. Put a title before a |, or leave it out and the file name is used. Lines starting with # are notes. An address can be a full web address or one relative to the list file, so a folder of routines can be moved without editing the list.

      # my routines
Stick Control page 5 | stick-control-page-5
Stick Control page 6 | stick-control-page-6
https://example.com/another-routine.txt

      JSON also works: ["file-one", {"title":"Page 6","url":"file-two"}], or an object with a routines list.

      Hosting one on GitHub

      • Put the list and the routine files in a repository, then open each file and use the Raw button. The address that opens is the one to use, starting with raw.githubusercontent.com.
      • Each routine file is a routine in the normal format, with its own title: line if you like. Without one, the title from the list is added.
      • The host has to allow other sites to read the file. GitHub's raw addresses do. Many other sites do not, and the app will say it could not reach the address.

      Scores, tempo and progress

      • On time is the share of due strokes that landed inside your on-time window. An exercise needs at least one full pass to be scored.
      • Exercise table: after a routine run, each exercise gets its own accuracy, average error and spread, plus your earlier best at that tempo.
      • The target is 90%. An exercise is "cleared" at a tempo when it reaches 90% there. After a run the app says whether every exercise cleared, and suggests +4 BPM if so. Otherwise it names the weak exercises and suggests staying at the same tempo. These are only suggestions. Any tempo is always allowed.
      • Discard this run removes a run from your records, for example after a bad microphone read.
      • Stats page: every pattern, weakest first, with accuracy at the tempo you last played, your fastest cleared tempo and your lead/drag bias. "Practise" loads that pattern. A routine counts as cleared at a tempo when one run covered every exercise and all reached 90%.
      • Practise weak exercises. After a routine run, a button plays only the exercises that were below the target. Restart repeats the same exercises. A run of only some exercises never counts as clearing the routine.
      • Tempo guidance. Each exercise in the start list shows the tempo it has cleared, or "new". The summary shows the tempo at which the whole routine has been cleared.
      • Records are saved in this browser only. Use Export to keep a copy or move to another device, and Import to bring one back. The app asks the browser to keep your records and reminds you to export a copy if you never have, or if the last one is two weeks old. On iPhone and iPad, Safari can clear a site's data after about a week without use, so add the page to your Home Screen.
      • History: on the Stats page, switch to History to see your last 300 runs, grouped by exercise or routine with the most recent group first. Tap a group for its runs, and a run for its exercises. Replay loads the same pattern or routine, tempo, click level and settings on the Play page, ready to start. Delete removes the run from your records. Runs older than the last 300 are folded into your totals, streaks, achievements and cleared tempos, which are kept for good, but they can no longer be replayed.

      Settings details

      • Mic check: in Settings, under Device, press Mic check, stay quiet for two seconds, then hit the pad ten times. It sets the trigger line and settle time, and tells you if the signal is too quiet, clipping, uneven or double triggering.
      • Trigger line: raise it if room noise counts as hits, and lower it if soft strokes are missed.
      • Stick calibration: hit the same spot of the pad with one stick only, at normal strength, until the count finishes. Do it for both sticks.
      • Test left vs right: after calibrating, press it and hit once with whichever hand is shown, 20 times in a shuffled order. It reports how often the app named the right stick, how each hand did, and how many hits would be tagged with the wrong hand in a run. Below 90% is worth recalibrating.
      • Latency: sound reaches the page late, and the click reaches your ears late. Press Auto calibrate once on each device. It plays clicks through the speakers and times when the mic hears them, so you don't play. Calibrate by playing is the second choice: hit right on each click and keep going until the number settles, then stop (80 hits or more). It assumes you were on the click on average. You can also type a number.
      • Click level: Easy clicks on every stroke (none on rests). Medium clicks on every beat. Hard clicks on beats 1 and 3, or every second beat from the start of the bar. Extreme clicks only on the first beat of each bar. Count-in only clicks just the count-in and is not scored in your stats. Every level is scored against the same grid, and each level is cleared on its own. Use headphones, or the click will be picked up as strokes.

      If something is off

      • The microphone will not turn on: the page needs a secure address. Opening the file from your computer works in Chrome and Edge. Otherwise serve it with python3 -m http.server or switch Input to Keyboard.
      • Every stroke looks late or early: run Calibrate latency.
      • Extra hits show up: raise the trigger line, or use headphones for the click.
      • Wrong-hand tags look random: calibrate both sticks again on the same spot of the pad.
